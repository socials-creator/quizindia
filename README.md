# India Map Quiz — States & Union Territories

A mobile-friendly, tap-the-map geography game based on the interaction model of the existing Where in the World pointing game.

## Files
- `index.html` — app structure and metadata
- `style.css` — responsive interface
- `script.js` — game logic, map rendering, progress, history and score

## Deploy to GitHub Pages
1. Create a new repository (or use a separate branch/repository for the India quiz).
2. Upload all files to the repository root.
3. In **Settings → Pages**, choose **Deploy from a branch**, select `main` and `/ (root)`.
4. Open the published GitHub Pages URL.

## Map and reference data
- Clickable boundaries: GeoJSON/TopoJSON from https://github.com/udit-001/india-maps-data, loaded via jsDelivr (with raw.githubusercontent fallback). Individual state/UT files fill in anything missing from the country file.
- v4.2 new-district borders (Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan): LGD district and sub-district boundaries from https://github.com/ramSeraph/indian_admin_boundaries, used offline to derive the patches embedded in `script.js`.
- Official political map reference and official state/UT map downloads: https://surveyofindia.gov.in/pages/political-map-of-india
- Union Territory list: Ministry of Home Affairs, https://www.mha.gov.in/union-territories-divisions

The app checks that all 28 States and 8 Union Territories are available before starting a round. D3.js and topojson-client are loaded from jsDelivr.

## Gameplay
Pick a mode with the switch at the top of the page:
- **States & UTs:** all 28 States + 8 Union Territories (36 regions).
- **Districts:** point out the districts of one state or of all the Union Territories. Choose from the dropdown under the mode switch (alphabetical; nothing is pre-selected). **All 28 States are available** — **Andhra Pradesh** (28), **Arunachal Pradesh** (27), **Assam** (35), **Bihar** (38), **Chhattisgarh** (33), **Goa** (3, incl. Kushavati), **Gujarat** (34), **Haryana** (23), **Himachal Pradesh** (12), **Jharkhand** (24), **Karnataka** (31), **Kerala** (14), **Madhya Pradesh** (55), **Maharashtra** (36), **Manipur** (16), **Meghalaya** (12), **Mizoram** (11), **Nagaland** (17), **Odisha** (30), **Punjab** (23), **Rajasthan** (41), **Sikkim** (6), **Tamil Nadu** (38), **Telangana** (33), **Tripura** (8), **Uttar Pradesh** (75), **Uttarakhand** (13), **West Bengal** (27) — and **Union Territories**, one combined map of Delhi (13), Jammu & Kashmir (22, incl. Muzaffarabad and Mirpur of Pakistan-occupied J&K, labelled PoJK), Ladakh (8, approximate outlines), Lakshadweep (10 sub-divisions), Puducherry (4), Andaman & Nicobar (3), Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu (3) and Chandigarh (1) — 64 in all, with the other states shown faintly for orientation.
- **Capitals:** tap each capital on the map. The question shows only the city (you are expected to know which state it serves); the state appears once you answer. 38 cities: the capitals of all 28 states and 8 UTs (Chandigarh serves Punjab, Haryana and the UT of Chandigarh, so there are 34 distinct primary cities) plus four second or seasonal capitals — Jammu (J&K, winter), Dharamshala (Himachal Pradesh, winter), Bhararisain (Gairsain) (Uttarakhand, summer) and Nagpur (Maharashtra, winter). Tap radius 45 km (30–35 km for Chandigarh, Mumbai, New Delhi and Gangtok).
- **Nature:** 61 features in 7 choices — Rivers (20), Mountain ranges (6), Major peaks (10), National parks (14), Lakes (9), Deserts & salt flats (2), or All 61. The 10 peaks are chosen from the highest and best-known peaks across the country, including claimed territory, with every pair at least 70 km apart: K2, Kangchenjunga, Nanga Parbat, Distaghil Sar, Nanda Devi, Kamet, Saser Kangri I, Nun, Anamudi and Guru Shikhar. Rivers and ranges are matched by distance to their line, peaks by 24 km, parks 45 km, lakes 35–45 km, deserts 90–150 km; where a peak or park lies on a range line, the point wins. Lakshadweep’s islands are drawn as in the other maps.
- **Learn:** study any of the 28 states and 8 Union Territories (the dropdown has just two groups: States and Union Territories, each alphabetical). Each shows capital, formation, main languages, number of districts, population (Census 2011), area, neighbours and a ‘known for’ line, with tappable districts, optional name labels, flashcards, and a button that starts the district quiz. For a UT the combined UT map zooms to it and dims the others.
- **Review mistakes (all modes):** every miss or skip is remembered per mode / state / category and cleared once you find it later; a *Review mistakes (n)* button starts a round with only those. Review rounds don't change your personal best. The info panel shows your accuracy per mode and the items still tricky; *Clear my progress* wipes it. Everything stays on the device.
- **Outlines (new in v3.2):** a single state/UT outline is shown on its own; type its 2-letter licence plate code and it submits automatically after 2 letters (on-screen letter keyboard on touch devices, physical keyboard on desktop). Outlines use the same boundary data as the States & UTs mode and follow light/dark mode. Current codes are shown in brackets in this mode and in States & UTs mode (e.g. Karnataka (KA)). Only the current code is accepted (e.g. Telangana TG, Odisha OD, Uttarakhand UK, Chhattisgarh CG, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu DD). The code table is `PLATE` in `script.js`.
- **Airports (new in v2.1):** point out 111 airports: India's international (incl. limited-international/customs) airports plus major domestic ones, and Warangal (Mamnoor, under redevelopment). Same-city/nearby airports use smaller tap radii (35–50 km) to limit overlap. The tap target is a ~60 km radius (35 km for same-city airports: Delhi/Noida, Mumbai/Navi Mumbai, Goa's two airports; the nearest airport within range wins) around each airport (`AIRPORT_RADIUS_KM` in `script.js`); tapping elsewhere counts as a miss. Found/missed airports reveal their tap circle. Edit the `AIRPORTS` list to add or remove airports.
- A round ends after every region in the mode is found or after 5 misses.
- Wrong taps and skips reveal the target region.
- Last 10 rounds are saved on the current device/browser. Best score is tracked separately for each mode, and your last-used mode is remembered.

## Adding more states to Districts mode
As of v4.2 every State and the Union Territories are covered; this section is for future changes (a new district, a rename) rather than missing states.
A set can also list several boundary files (`sources`, see "Union Territories"), a `parents` map (shown as "District of …"), faint `context` states, a `split` (cut one district into two along a line) and `cuts` (v4.0+: a list of `{from, newName, region}` entries, where `from` may be one parent or an array of parents that carve a newer district out of its parent with an approximate outline polygon; works for any shape, see `cutRegions` in `script.js`).
In `script.js`, add an entry to `DISTRICT_SETS` (state name, boundary-file `slug`, the official district list, and an `aliases` map for older/variant spellings). The dropdown entry, boundary loading, scoring and best-score tracking pick it up automatically. District names are read from the boundary file, so the number of districts in a round follows the data.

## Important
This is an educational quiz, not a legal or survey-grade boundary product. Map service availability, CORS policy, and map data revisions are controlled by the service provider. Check the official Survey of India reference map for authoritative use.
